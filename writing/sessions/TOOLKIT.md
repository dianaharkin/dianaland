# THE FOUR TOOLS — what each one is, and why she needs it

For Lauren, to teach from. Plain-language definitions Diana can repeat back.
Order matters: each one is useless to her until the one before it is real.

---

## 1. CLAUDE — the engine
**Get it:** claude.ai → app store, iOS and Android. Free tier works to start; Pro if she
wants Projects to hold the full corpus comfortably. Sign in with the same email everywhere.

**Say it like this:** "It's where your writing lives and where the drafts come from."

**What she actually needs to be able to do:** open the project, send a photo into it,
read what comes back, and copy text out. That's it. Everything else is optional.

**Watch for:** she starts a new chat outside the project and wonders why it doesn't sound
like her. Teach the difference between a chat and a project chat once, clearly, session 1.

---

## 2. BUFFER — the queue
**Get it:** buffer.com → app store. Free plan covers three channels, ten posts queued each.
Connect Instagram first, only Instagram, until that's a habit.

**Say it like this:** "You write on Sunday. Buffer posts it on Tuesday. You don't touch your
phone on Tuesday."

**Why it matters more than it looks:** it breaks the link between writing and posting.
Right now publishing means opening Instagram, which means seeing Instagram. The queue means
she can write for an hour and not be online at all. For someone whose material is this personal,
that separation is most of what makes it sustainable.

**Watch for:** she'll want to post directly "just this once". Fine occasionally. If it becomes
every time, the queue dies and the whole rhythm goes with it.

---

## 3. GITHUB — the safe copy
**Get it:** github.com → free account. Desktop app is optional; the website is enough at first.

**Say it like this:** "It's where your files are kept, with every version you've ever had.
Nothing you write can be lost, and nothing can be overwritten by accident."

**Why she needs it:** the corpus is years of her journals. It cannot live only in a Claude
project and only on her phone. It also becomes the thing Vercel builds her site from.

**Non-negotiable:** the corpus repo is **PRIVATE**. Set it private at creation, confirm it
together on screen, and tell her plainly what public would have meant. A private repo she
understands is worth more than a private repo she was told about.

**Watch for:** git is the single most likely place to lose her. Do not teach branches, merges,
or the command line. Website only: edit file, write what changed, commit. Three clicks.

---

## 4. VERCEL — the live site
**Get it:** vercel.com → sign in *with GitHub*. That link is what makes the rest work.

**Say it like this:** "You change a file on GitHub. A minute later the website has changed.
That's all Vercel is."

**Why it matters to her specifically:** dianaland already runs on it. She's been watching
Lauren deploy her app for weeks. Teaching her this turns her app from something being made
*for* her into something she owns.

**Watch for:** deploys fail in ways that look catastrophic and aren't. Break one on purpose
in front of her, read the error together, fix it. If she sees a red failure once with someone
next to her, she won't panic at it alone at 11pm.

---

## The order, and why

Claude → Buffer → GitHub → Vercel.

Value lands first, technical risk lands last. By the time she meets git she has already
published things she's proud of, so the boring part is in service of something real. Doing
it in the other order — repos and deploys before she's ever posted — is how people decide
this isn't for them.
