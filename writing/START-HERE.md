# START HERE — Diana

You already write every day. This doesn't ask you to write more.
It asks you to photograph what you already wrote.

That's the whole thing. Everything else is downstream of that.

---

## What you're setting up

A project inside the Claude app on your phone that has read your journals and
knows how you write. You send it a photo of a page. It gives you back something
you could publish, in your own voice, not in wellness-Instagram voice.

It gets better every time you feed it a page. Genuinely better — not "the AI learns"
in a hand-wavy way. There's a file that describes how you write, and every ten pages
we rewrite that file from everything it's seen. You'll be able to watch it sharpen.

## What to say to it

You don't need to write good prompts. Say the short thing:

- send a photo of a page → *it transcribes it and adds it to what it knows about you*
- **"make this a post"** → one draft, in your voice
- **"caption this"** + a photo → one to three lines
- **"newsletter"** → the longer version
- **"does this sound like me?"** + paste your own writing → it scores it and tells you the truth
- **"what should I write about"** → three prompts from your own pages
- **"matrix"** → the whole grid: your five subjects crossed with eight ways in, forty posts
- **"deal me a card"** → one object off your own website, and three ways to write it
- **"polaroid post"** → a polaroid, its caption, and the post that's already 60% written
- **"refresh the voice"** → after about ten new pages, it rewrites what it knows about you

---

## Two things it will always do

**It scores itself.** Every draft ends with something like `Voice check: 9/10 - no unflattering
admission`. If it's guessing, you'll see it guessing. Don't trust a draft that doesn't score itself.

**It holds things back.** Some of what you write is not for the internet — your sobriety,
your father, your body. It won't draft those for an audience unless you tell it to, in that
conversation, out loud. That's yours to decide every time, not something it assumes.
It'll still sometimes lift one line off a private page and offer you that line on its own.

---

## The only habit that matters

Photograph the page. Not every page. Not neatly. Just, when you write something
that felt like something — take the photo before you close the book.

Ten seconds. That's the entire ask.
