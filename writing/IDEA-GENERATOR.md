# THE IDEA GENERATOR — Diana

You do not have an idea problem. You have an inventory problem.

There are 84 of your photographs on your own website. Fifty-six of them have
your handwriting on the back. There are six films, four rooms, a lion, a
candle, a doll, and a video of you singing straight down the lens for two
minutes and fifty-two seconds. Every one of those is a post you have already
made and not yet published.

This file is two things stacked. The bottom layer is a deck of your own
material. The top layer is a grid that turns any one item in the deck into
eight different posts. Together they are more ideas than you will use.

---

## PART 1 — THE FIVE PILLARS

Everything you publish sits in one of five buckets. Not five topics you
*should* cover. Five things your own pages already keep coming back to.

**Three to five is the cap, and it is deliberate.** More than five and none
of them ever becomes the thing you're known for.

| # | Pillar | What lives here | Receipt |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The road and the room** | Playing live. Load-in, soundcheck, the green room, the crowd that came for someone else, the drive home at 2am. | Woodside festival, the band set, the acoustic set, the club stage — five films in the black book |
| **2** | **Making the thing** | Writing, practicing, the take that isn't working, the song that arrived in a form you didn't order. | the heart: "Come Away With Me," 2:52, one take, straight to camera |
| **3** | **The body keeps the schedule** | Being an artist inside a body that has opinions. Tired, sore, restless, fed, unfed, walked home. | *"today I am restless and tired. Sore but strong."* |
| **4** | **Wanting things out loud** | Ambition, money, approval, envy, the embarrassing motive named plainly. Your strongest register and the one most people flinch from. | *"I can't say right now that I don't crave the approval but I do like it, I do want it, it does give me pleasure."* |
| **5** | **Woodside** | Place as material. The errand, the iced coffee, the window seat, the moon on the walk home, the neighbourhood you actually live in. | *"The coffee was rich, earthy and delicious - It was just as I wanted."* |

**If a draft doesn't belong to one of these, don't publish it.** That is the
entire filtering rule and it is worth more than any idea below it.

## PART 2 — THE EIGHT ANGLES

Justin Welsh's Content Matrix, in his eight fixed angles. His definitions are
in quotes. What follows each is what it means for a working musician, because
the matrix was written for people who sell software and it needs translating.

**1 · Actionable** — *"Ultra-specific how-to. Teaches the reader to do one thing."*
> How you actually do a small piece of your job. Not "how to be a musician."
> How you warm up in a car. How you pick which song goes second.

**2 · Motivational** — *"Inspirational story about someone who did something extraordinary in the niche."*
> Careful here. Yours is never the polished version. Yours is somebody who
> kept going in a specific, unglamorous, dated way — including when the
> somebody is you, told without the trophy at the end.

**3 · Analytical** — *"Breakdown of why something works the way it does."*
> Why a room goes quiet at a particular moment. Why a song lands live and
> dies on a recording. You know a hundred of these and assume everyone does.

**4 · Contrarian** — *"Go against the common advice in the niche and back it up."*
> The advice musicians repeat to each other that you have watched be wrong.
> This is Move 2 territory — it only works if you name what it cost you to
> find out.

**5 · Observation** — *"A hidden, silent, or underdiscussed trend the user has noticed."*
> The thing you keep noticing at shows and have never said out loud because
> you assumed it was just you.

**6 · X vs Y** — *"Compare two entities (tools, styles, frameworks, companies)."*
> Two venues. Two versions of the same song. The set you play for a room that
> came to see you versus a room that didn't. Playing at 8pm versus 11pm.

**7 · Present vs Future** — *"Current state vs a specific prediction, with the why."*
> Where you are now, where you think this goes, and why. Dated. Specific
> enough to be wrong.

**8 · Listicle** — *"A list of resources, tips, mistakes, lessons, or steps."*
> Your list, from your own pages, with the numbers real. "Six things in my
> bag that aren't the instrument." Never a round number you rounded.

## PART 3 — THE MATRIX

Five pillars × eight angles = **forty posts**, and that is before the deck in
Part 4.

Ask the project: **"matrix"** — and it fills the grid with one specific,
concrete headline per cell, drawn from your corpus, not from general advice.
Or ask for one cell by name: **"pillar 4, contrarian."**

Worked examples, one per pillar, so the shape is clear:

| Pillar × Angle | The idea |
|---|---|
| **1 · Road** × Actionable | The exact thing you do in the ten minutes between load-in and doors |
| **2 · Making** × Analytical | Why the take you kept was the one with the mistake in it |
| **3 · Body** × Contrarian | Playing tired is not always worse, and here is the night that proved it |
| **4 · Wanting** × Observation | What you have noticed about which musicians ask for money and which don't |
| **5 · Woodside** × X vs Y | Writing at the window seat versus writing at the kitchen table, and what each one produces |

### The rule that keeps the grid from producing sludge

**Every cell must name one physical thing.** A room, a drink, a bag, a
staircase, a temperature, a time on a clock. If the post could be written from
an armchair without remembering anything specific, the cell is empty and the
engine should say so rather than fill it.

That rule comes straight out of your own pages: the insight is never
announced first. It is earned from something you could photograph.

## PART 4 — THE DIANALAND DECK

This is the part no framework gives you. Your website is a physical inventory
of content, and every object in it is a story you have already decided is
worth keeping.

**How to use it: pick a card, pick an angle, write.**

### The entrance
The fork. A door for people who want the whole thing, and a plain button for
people who just need the phone number. → *Why you built an easy way to skip
the art you made.* (Contrarian, Analytical)

### The forest
- **The lion.** Tap and your song starts. Tap again and it stops. It follows
  you out of the forest and through the whole world. → *A song that doesn't
  stay in the room you started it in.* (Observation, Analytical)
- **The apparition.** → *Being visible and not-quite-there at the same time.* (Pillar 4)
- **The polaroids on the trees.** 56 backs in your handwriting.

### The sea
- **The water.** Real ripples, real sun on it, redrawn every frame. → *What
  blue hour does to a person.* (Pillar 5, Observation)
- **She Surfaces.** → the whole arc of coming up for air, in one hotspot.

### The funhouse
- **The mirrors.** → *X vs Y, structurally.* Every version of yourself the
  room hands back.
- **The doll — before and after.** Two photographs, one fading into the other,
  the new one holding twice as long. → *the single strongest visual you own
  for Pillar 4.* Post the crossfade. Say the unflattering part.
- **The heart.** You, 2:52, straight to camera, "Come Away With Me." No
  master, no licence, no band. → *Why the version with nothing behind it is
  the one on the site.* (Contrarian, Motivational)

### The temple
- **The candle and the poem.** → the only place on the site where you are a
  writer and not a musician.
- **The photo album.** Eight pages. → eight posts, minimum.
- **The black book.** Bio, press, five performance films. → Pillar 1, all of it.
- **The bowls.** → sound as something other than performance.

### The television
Eight videos, three of them public, the rest shorts and unlisted. → *Why the
ones you didn't publish are on your site.* (Observation, Contrarian)

### The polaroids
**Fifty-six captions in your own handwriting.** This is the richest seam in
the whole deck and the least work. One polaroid, photographed, plus the line
on its back, plus one sentence of what you'd add now. That is a finished post
in ninety seconds, and there are fifty-six of them.

### The pool
Eighty-four photographs, dealing themselves into rooms at random. Twenty-eight
still have blank backs. → *Every blank back is an open prompt: what was
happening in this one.*

## PART 5 — HOW TO ASK FOR IT

On the phone, in a minute, one of these:

| Say | You get |
|---|---|
| **"what should I write about"** | three prompts from your own pages, each naming a physical thing |
| **"matrix"** | the full 5 × 8 grid, one headline per cell |
| **"pillar 3, listicle"** | one cell, three options |
| **"deal me a card"** | one object from the Dianaland deck + three angles on it |
| **"polaroid post"** | pick a polaroid, get the post its caption is already 60% of |
| **"I filmed something"** *(+ paste a transcript)* | the two or three posts hiding in what you said out loud |
| **"what haven't I written about"** | the pillar that's gone quiet, and why that matters |

### From the transcript shortcut

Video → share sheet → **Transcript** → copy → paste here → *"make this a post."*
You talk better than you type when you're tired. That path exists for exactly
those days.

## PART 6 — THE RULES ON THIS FILE

**1 · The voice file outranks this one.** An idea that can only be written in
wellness-Instagram voice is a dead idea. Kill it, don't translate it. The
banned list in `LEXICON.md` applies to every headline generated here.

**2 · Never invent a register the corpus hasn't seen.** If she asks for funny
and type 9 in `COVERAGE.md` is still empty, say so before drafting, draft the
best available version, and mark it a guess. Guessing quietly is how the voice
drifts.

**3 · Privacy gates hold at the idea stage, not just the draft stage.**
Sobriety, her father, her body as a private subject — those do not get
generated as *ideas* for an audience either, unless she opens the door in that
conversation. An idea list is not a loophole.

**4 · One idea per post.** If a headline has an "and" in it, it is two posts.

**5 · No round numbers.** If the list has seven things, it is seven things.
Six is not tidier, it is wrong.

**6 · Every generated idea names its cell.** `[Pillar 2 × Contrarian]` or
`[Deck: the doll × Observation]` at the end of the line. So she can see which
seams she keeps returning to and which she never touches.
